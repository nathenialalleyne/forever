A public release should either omit the generated reports, regenerate a deliberately sanitized report format, or obtain a specific legal review and add explicit per-path provenance and licensing.

## Legal facts from the CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0 text

The official legal code checked was [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/legalcode.en), and the official deed was [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/). The following are licence-text facts, not a legal opinion:

- Section 1 defines **Share** as providing material to the public by means such as “reproduction, public display, public performance, distribution, dissemination, communication, or importation,” and making it available for members of the public to access at a place and time they choose.
- Section 1 defines **NonCommercial** as activity “not primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage or monetary compensation.” The same section says digital file-sharing without payment is an example of NonCommercial, but that example does not resolve every repository, sponsorship, advertising, or distribution context.
- Section 2(a)(1) grants the rights to reproduce and Share the licensed material, in whole or in part, for NonCommercial purposes only, and to produce, reproduce, and Share Adapted Material for NonCommercial purposes only.
- Section 3(a) requires attribution information when the material is Shared, including creator identification where supplied, copyright notice, licence notice, disclaimer notice, the licence URI where practical, and an indication of modifications.
- Section 3(b) requires Adapted Material to be licensed under the same licence elements, a later version, or a listed compatible licence, with the licence notice and no additional restrictions that limit the granted rights.
- Section 2(a)(5)(C) says the licensee may not offer or impose additional or different terms or apply effective technological measures that restrict the rights granted by the licence.
- Section 4 applies the NonCommercial and attribution conditions to extraction, reuse, reproduction, and sharing of all or a substantial portion of a database protected by sui generis database rights, and it can treat an adapted database as Adapted Material.

The official deed summarises the same structure as attribution, noncommercial use, share-alike licensing for adaptations, and no additional restrictions.

## Audit reading, not legal advice

My reading of those terms is:

- Committing the generated directory to a public Git repository is very likely an act of making material available to the public, and therefore presents a strong risk of being a Share under Section 1. The 1,558 exact source-line occurrences plus nine normalized command-text fields mean the directory is more than an index or a link.
- Whether individual Minecraft command lines, facts, identifiers, the selection and arrangement of the report, or the database-like inventory are protected works, and whether a particular report is Adapted Material, requires a lawyer familiar with the upstream work and applicable law. This audit does not decide those questions.
- The root Forever licence says it applies to original Forever material and excludes third-party material. That is helpful intent, but the generated directory has no field-level or directory-level notice that a public reader can use to identify the CC material and its conditions. A public reader could still reasonably see the root All-Rights-Reserved presentation as restricting copying of the generated files.
- If the generated output is Adapted Material, putting it under a non-CC All-Rights-Reserved restriction would be a significant ShareAlike/no-additional-restrictions conflict. Changing the root licence for original code cannot silently relicense Matcha or cure that conflict.
- A public repository is not automatically commercial merely because it is public. However, project funding, advertising, paid distribution, commercial pack hosting, or other facts could make the intended use primarily commercial. A lawyer should decide this before any release that has commercial advantage or monetary compensation.
- The reports may contain material from the Matcha archive that itself has additional provenance or contributor rights. The audit verified the Matcha release identity and report provenance, not every copyright claim inside the upstream archive.

This is an evidence-led risk assessment, not legal advice or a declaration that a public repository would certainly infringe. Legal review is genuinely warranted if the reports or direct excerpts are retained, if the project will be monetized, or if a combined modpack will be redistributed.

## URL/hash pinning versus binary redistribution

Packwiz metadata is not a copy of a JAR. The current `pack/` files contain official download URLs, hashes, versions, and environment metadata. The current export `dist/many-roads-home-0.1.0-dev.mrpack` is 2,965 bytes and contains only `modrinth.index.json` plus an `overrides/defaultconfigs/global_packs.toml` file. Its uncompressed contents total 7,022 bytes and contain no JAR or Matcha ZIP. The index has seven dependency entries with download URLs, hashes, sizes, and environment flags.

My audit reading, not legal advice, is that merely describing an upstream file by URL and hash is materially different from rehosting or embedding that file. It does not grant permission to copy, modify, or redistribute the upstream file, and it does not remove platform terms or upstream licence conditions. It also does not make the current notices complete.

If a release host, launcher, mirror, cache, or pack archive bundles the JARs or Matcha ZIP, that is a different distribution surface. It becomes necessary to review each upstream licence, notices, source/relinking obligations, permission-only material, platform terms, and any alteration or repackaging. In particular:

- Global Packs cannot be treated as cleared merely because the `.mrpack` points to its official URL.
- Jade and Matcha's NonCommercial/ShareAlike terms must be compatible with the intended pack host, funding, and use.
- Effortless Building's embedded MCP warning must be resolved before shipping that JAR.
- A `.mrpack` that contains only URL/hash references reduces direct binary rehosting, but it is not a blanket publication approval.

## Source repository versus modpack distribution

These are separate release decisions:

### Public source repository

A source repository could contain original Java, data, scripts, documentation, Packwiz metadata, and the standalone audit tool without containing third-party JARs. Before publication, it still needs:

1. A deliberate choice of rights for original Forever code and assets. The current bespoke licence is All-Rights-Reserved and is not an open-source licence.
2. A reviewed boundary for Matcha-derived reports, direct command quotations, Matcha-specific docs, and adapter identifiers.
3. A clean public history if generated Matcha output is excluded. The current reachable history exposes commit `0f09a38`.
4. Complete dependency notices and contributor/source provenance.
5. A decision about whether the repository is public read-only proprietary source or an open-source project. Public visibility alone does not grant reuse rights.

The technically simplest low-risk source layout is to keep the audit tool and lock/hash/count evidence, omit generated Matcha reports and direct excerpts from the public snapshot, and document how a legitimately obtained archive can regenerate them. That approach still needs a legal/provenance review of the authored Matcha analysis docs.

### Modpack distribution

A modpack release is a combined distribution of a pack definition, configuration, datapacks/resource packs, and dependencies obtained or delivered through a host. Before release, it needs:

1. An official host/platform and a review of its pack and dependency terms.
2. Complete attribution and licence notices for every dependency and bundled component.
3. Permission or replacement for Global Packs, plus resolution of the Effortless MCP warning.
4. A documented noncommercial/share-alike plan for Matcha and Jade, including any upstream content inside those projects.
5. A reproducible export check confirming whether the `.mrpack` contains only URL/hash references or bundled binaries.
6. No claim that Packwiz metadata changes third-party ownership or permissions.

A source repository can avoid committing binaries while a distribution host still rehosts or supplies them. Passing one does not automatically clear the other.

## Publication blockers

The following blockers must be resolved or explicitly accepted before publication:

- **Rights model:** decide whether the public repository is proprietary read-only, open source, or accompanied by a distributable modpack. The current All-Rights-Reserved licence is not an open-source grant.
- **Generated Matcha history:** decide whether the 21 reports and direct excerpts are retained under a legally reviewed separate boundary or omitted from a clean public history. The existing history is reachable from the active branches and tag.
- **Attribution/provenance:** add a complete, path-aware notice for Matcha-derived output, Matcha-facing docs, adapter material, all adopted/piloted dependencies, and build-tool binaries.
- **Upstream release gates:** resolve Global Packs ARR, the Effortless MCP warning, and the commercial/noncommercial/share-alike implications of Matcha and Jade.
- **Distribution model:** decide whether a public `.mrpack` will contain only download references or rehost/bundle files. Review the chosen platform and any commercial use separately.

## Follow-up required

1. The owner chooses the intended public rights model and whether commercial use or monetized hosting is contemplated.
2. A lawyer reviews the retained Matcha-derived reports/quotations, the report database/selection issue, CC-BY-NC-SA ShareAlike compatibility with the root licence, and the intended commercial context if any.
3. Create a release-only provenance/notice manifest that separates original Forever material, Matcha-derived material, synthetic fixtures, and each dependency.
4. For a source-only public repository, create a clean reviewed public history or perform an explicitly approved history rewrite. Do not rely on deleting files at the tip.
5. For a modpack release, obtain Global Packs permission or replace it; resolve the MCP-data notice; and complete the dependency/host review before publishing an archive or mirror.
